Five tracks

Pick one when you register

Your track shapes who you're matched with and how your work is read. Cross-track projects are welcome — just tell us where yours primarily sits. The examples are starting points, not assignments.

01

Who gets left behind, and how fast

Not whether AI displaces workers, but who, where, on what timeline, and whether anyone is measuring it in real time.

  • Displacement models by sector or income bracket
  • Early-warning systems from job-posting data
  • Honest evaluations of retraining or wage subsidies
  • Base rates from past technological transitions

Labor economics · alternative data · program evaluation

02

Who controls AI, and what that buys them

A handful of firms control the most capable systems on earth. Concentration of capability is concentration of power. Name it, measure it, propose a remedy that survives scrutiny.

  • Antitrust analysis of the current AI stack
  • Models of winner-take-all dynamics
  • Stress-tests of breakups or interoperability mandates
  • Comparative study of government responses

Industrial organization · antitrust · comparative regulation

03

The Global South is not an afterthought

The countries most exposed to disruption are barely in the conversation. The global distribution of AI's impact is a choice, not an inevitability. Make the choice visible.

  • Country analysis a policymaker could use
  • Remittance flows under labor displacement
  • Technology diffusion across economic contexts
  • Mechanisms that give developing countries leverage

Development economics · trade and remittances · country expertise

04

What levers governments actually have

Everyone has a proposal. Almost nobody has checked whether it works, under what conditions, at what cost. Pick one and do the honest evaluation.

  • UBI costed with every assumption stated
  • Sovereign AI fund design with real governance
  • Redistribution under rapid productivity growth
  • Compute governance against realistic evasion

Public finance · mechanism design · simulation

05

We can't manage what we can't see

GDP was built for an industrial economy and moves in quarters when policy needs months. Build the instruments that would let anyone watch this happen in real time.

  • Real-time economic indicators
  • Working dashboards on alternative data
  • New ways to measure AI productivity gains
  • Critiques of GDP with concrete replacements

Measurement · data engineering · dashboards

Judging

A narrow question answered honestly beats a broad one gestured at

Every submission is read by at least two judges against the same four criteria. Everyone gets written feedback.

25%

Question and scope

A specific question, narrow enough to actually answer in three days. "How will AI affect work" scores badly. "Did postings for entry-level translation roles fall faster than a matched control after mid-2023" scores well.

30%

Rigor and honesty

Assumptions on the surface, methods a reader can check, limitations named rather than buried. A model with three stated wrong assumptions beats one with three hidden ones.

25%

Usefulness

Could someone act on this on Monday? A number, a method, a dataset, a decision rule — something that moves past what's already been said.

20%

Communication

Can a smart person outside your discipline follow it? Clear charts, defined terms, and a conclusion that says what you found. Judges score clarity, not polish.

Questions

The things people actually ask

Do I need a background in AI or economics?

No. Curiosity and a willingness to narrow a question are the requirements. Several tracks lean more on policy, development, or measurement expertise than on either AI or economics. If you can read a paper in your own field and tell good work from bad, you can contribute here.

Do I need a team?

No — that's rather the point. Register on your own and we build a team around you from your track, your background, and when you're free. You can also register with people you already know, up to five.

Can I submit on my own?

Yes, though we'd rather you didn't. Solo submissions are judged on the same criteria as team ones, with no allowance for having had fewer hands.

How much time does this actually take?

Most people put in 15–25 hours across the three days. You can do something real in less if you scope tightly. You cannot do something real in five.

What exactly am I submitting?

Whatever form fits the work: a written brief, an interactive site, a model, a dataset, a dashboard, a playable simulation, or some combination. Build the thing that makes your point best.

Alongside it, every team submits a short write-up — two pages or so — that stands on its own: the question, what you did, what you found, what you're not claiming. This is what judges read first, and it's what makes submissions comparable when one team ships a game and another ships a regression. If your main artifact is already a written brief, that is your write-up.

If your artifact can't be opened in a browser — a local app, a notebook needing setup, anything with dependencies — include a three-minute video showing it working. Judges won't debug your environment.

Can I use AI tools?

Yes, for anything: coding, analysis, drafting, literature search. Say so in your submission and be specific about what you used it for. What matters is that you can defend every claim you make — if you can't explain how a number was produced, don't include it.

Can I use work I'd already started?

Yes, with disclosure. State what existed before Friday and what you built during the sprint. Judges score the new contribution.

Who owns what I make?

You do. We may ask to feature winning work publicly, and we'll ask before doing it.

What if my team goes quiet?

Tell us and we'll help — either backfill someone or move you. This happens at every hackathon, and it's much easier to fix on Friday than on Sunday.

What if my analysis fails on Saturday night?

Submit anyway. A well-documented negative result, or an honest account of why a method didn't work, is a real contribution and scores far better than nothing. Have a fallback in mind before you need it.

Is there a cost?

No.

Resources

Nobody should lose Friday night hunting for data

You don't need any of this to take part. It's here because half of all weekend projects die on "we couldn't get the data." Confirm your source is reachable before you commit to a question — an actual file, downloaded and opened, not "there should be data on this."

General

  • FRED — most US macro series, with an API
  • World Bank Open Data — cross-country indicators
  • OECD Data Explorer — productivity and labour detail
  • Our World in Data — cleaned and well-sourced
  • IPUMS — harmonised microdata; registration takes time, start early

01 — Displacement

  • O*NET — task and skill data by occupation
  • BLS — employment, wages, projections
  • Indeed Hiring Lab — job postings, higher frequency than official stats
  • For retraining evidence, DOL's commissioned evaluations of WIOA and Trade Adjustment Assistance are the rigorous source.

02 — Concentration

  • SEC EDGAR — filings, equity stakes, segment revenue
  • Epoch AI — compute and training run data
  • Stanford AI Index — investment and capability baselines
  • Provider pricing pages and their archived versions — API prices over time are recoverable and nobody has assembled them properly.

03 — Global South

  • ILOSTAT — employment by occupation for most countries
  • KNOMAD — bilateral remittance flows
  • UN Comtrade — trade in services by category
  • For a country brief, the national labour force survey usually beats any international aggregate.

04 — Policy levers

  • IMF — fiscal data and Article IV country reports
  • Penn World Table — long-run productivity and capital stock
  • Read the actual regulatory texts — the EU AI Act and equivalents elsewhere are all published.
  • Existing UBI pilot evaluations give you real effect sizes instead of assumed ones.

05 — Measurement

  • BEA — national accounts and the methodology docs that explain what GDP misses
  • The national accounts literature on digital and free goods is the right foundation for anything measuring consumer surplus.
  • Found a source we've missed? Post it in the channel so everyone gets it.
